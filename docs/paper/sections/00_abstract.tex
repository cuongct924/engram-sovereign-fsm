Modular blockchains obtain settlement and data availability (DA) from
independently operated layers. A chain whose block validity requires Bitcoin
settlement receipts and DA receipts inherits the liveness of those layers: when
either stalls, no proposal is valid and the chain halts although its validators
are connected and correct. Dropping the requirement restores progress but
suspends the guarantees it provided. We present \emph{Engram}, a protocol layer
on a lightly modified CometBFT core that makes this suspension an agreed,
deterministic state transition. Validators sign height-bound observations of
Bitcoin, DA and peer health as vote extensions; a \emph{health certificate}
aggregates them so that every metric stays within the range of the honest
observations (Lemma~\ref{lem:honest-range}), and a four-state finite state machine
evaluates it. In the degraded modes the chain keeps producing blocks, relaxes the
receipt requirement of the failed layer and excludes withdrawal-marked
transactions. It returns to \textsc{Anchored} only after hysteresis, DA backfill of the
degraded interval and a validator-verified recovery proof over all but a bounded
unproven tail. A layered TLA+ specification passes bounded model checking at two
layers (\resFSMStatesM{} distinct states in the largest run); the refinement of the
composed layer remains open. On a seven-validator testbed with Bitcoin regtest and
Celestia, Engram kept \resRQTwoEngram{} of its healthy block rate under a checkpoint
pause, a DA outage and both, where a receipt-gated baseline that reads receipts at each
validator kept \resRQTwoOob{} and one that reads them through vote extensions stopped
committing \resInBandHalt\,s after the fault began and did not resume. A composite
fault repeated five times, on one host and on seven machines in three regions, ended in
\textsc{Anchored} with complete AppHash agreement. The block interval grows by
\resOverheadPct{} on one host (95\% interval \resOverheadCI{}) and is 1.9 times
vanilla's at 100\,tx/s across regions. The price is the detection delay: \resExposure{}
withdrawal-marked transactions committed before the lock (40--125\,s on the testbed,
47--62\,min when converted to mainnet Bitcoin cadence by a model, not measured on mainnet), and none after it.
